Interface guidelines

What the Desk's interface is made of, when each piece is used, and what it means. Build a new screen from these pieces; do not invent another look for a job one of them already does. Every example here is the Desk's own component in its own stylesheet, so this page is what the Desk looks like.

Tabs and switchers

Picked by what the switch means, never by how much room there is.

Main menu

Where you are on the Desk: its places, once, in the header. The place you are on is filled; nothing else is marked. A count says how many are still open there, grey; Requests' turns amber while one waits for an answer.

On the Desk: the header.

Segmented control

Another view or filter of the same content: a list's filters, a mockup's pages, light and dark. A count sits inside its segment.

On the Desk: every list's filter, the review's light and dark.

Underline tabs with a number chip

Things reviewed one at a time: a request's design options. The chip carries the review:

  • ink for the one shown,
  • green for the approved one, even when shown,
  • amber while changes asked for on it wait for its next version; never on an approved or closed request, which draws none,
  • grey for the rest.

They stay one row and never scroll: a tab shrinks to fit, its name cut with an ellipsis on the baseline; what still does not fit folds into +n at the row's end.

On the Desk: a request's design options, in both modes, and the design workspace's. A pull request's changes are tiles, as All elements are.

Plain underline tabs

The sections of a panel or a page: each holds different content. A count is grey, beside the name.

On the Desk: the pages of Product and of the other tabs, and the sections of the design workspace's look panel.

Stepper

Variants of one thing in an order: an element's states, a size, a value one step up or down. The arrows step; the middle names where you are.

On the Desk: the design workspace's look panel and its states; an element's states in its dialog, with a pick in the middle, and the option's elements in its header.

Hover

Pictures to pick from

Every variant of one thing at once, as small pictures in a fold under the large one: a click shows it in the large picture, and the stepper above it agrees. The one shown is ringed in ink, its name in ink.

On the Desk: an element's All states in its dialog, in simple mode; full mode rings the state's card the same way.

A property's value

In the look panel, one value of a part's property: a segmented row for up to four short values, chips for more, a swatch for a colour. The picked one is filled or ringed in the accent.

On the Desk: the design workspace's look panel.

Version links

On the Desk: an approved request's spec.

Choice in a form

Picking one value a form needs, when the choice changes what the form asks next. Every option is a whole button with its words; the picked one is ringed in ink.

On the Desk: a new request's kind (full mode), an issue's kind, a live prototype's mode, Send to Slack's mode.

A small number to pick

A count from a short range, as one row of whole buttons that are pressed (aria-pressed), as a segmented control: the picked one filled in ink. Zero gets words that say what it does, never "None"; its line under the row says the rest. On a phone the row takes the form's width and its label goes above it.

On the Desk: simple mode's New request, its Design options.

Buttons

One primary action in a view

The dark primary is the next step. Green is only what approves an item: Approve, Approve for dev, Send to developers, and Looks good. Propose and Send to design only send it on to the next review: outlined, with a paper plane. Submit feedback wears the colour of the verdict it sends: green an approval, a quiet amber Request changes (a pale fill, amber words), ink Comment only. Everything else is neutral. Red only for what deletes.

An action keeps its name everywhere: “Request changes” in the header, in its dialog, and in the message that follows.

Small buttons sit inside a row, a card or a toolbar; the full size stands on its own.

A ghost (.btn.ghost, no fill and no edge until hovered) is a quiet move beside the real ones, one that only shows something, such as Read the spec; it never sends, approves or changes anything.

A way into the design workspace looks the same wherever it stands: its icon, on a very light violet fill (--ws at 7%, a violet edge), the words and the icon in the button's own ink.

One action, more than one way

When the main action of a form can go two ways, it is one split button: the action on its face, an arrow beside it that lists the ways, each a bold name and a muted line on what follows. The face keeps the last way picked; the ways are only those the person may take. With one way it is a plain button.

A form's main buttons stand large (40 px high) when the form is the whole task.

On the Desk: New request's Create draft / Create proposal or Send to design.

An icon alone, for a tool the list always has

A tool every visit uses, such as a list's filter, can be its icon alone: a 32 px square, the icon 17 px, neutral, at the end of its row. Its name and tooltip say what it does and what it is set to.

While it holds a setting that hides something, an accent dot sits on its border's upper right corner. Never a dark ring: that marks only the pressed segment of a switcher.

Colour

Neutral by default

Colour only for a state, a warning, the one primary action, or the reader's own comments: an ink means a state (who has the move, approved, live, closed), never decoration. Never a near copy of a token. Text on a coloured fill is a token that turns with the theme (--bg, --panel or --accent-ink), never a fixed colour such as #fff, so it reads in light and dark.

Folds

One style for every long read that starts closed

A line above, the name as a heading, + and − at the right. What the page is for stays open; the long reads fold.

Original request

What the person asked for, word for word, with their pictures.

What changed and why

The change, section by section.

What opens in place wears a chevron that turns

A button or a row that shows its content right there, under or beside it, ends its words with a chevron: pointing right while closed, turned down while open, as its aria-expanded says. Its words say what opens: Read the spec, then Hide the spec. A long read in the page's flow is a fold, with + and −, instead; a button that opens a panel over the page points down (Submit feedback).

Text

Every label earns its place

  • No sentence that explains a heading.
  • No label on a value that explains itself.
  • Dates without the year when it is this year.
  • Sentence case; capitals with spacing only head a group.

Dialogs and forms

A dialog asks one thing

Its name is the action that opened it, and its main button says the action again. A label sits above its field, grey and short. Nothing submits a form: every button is type="button".

Close is ✕, alone, in its top-right corner, named "Close" for a screen reader; never "✕ Close". Esc closes it too, and ✕ does what Esc does: a form with something typed or picked in it asks before it is lost; a dialog that is not a form (an element's) keeps what was being written for the next time it opens. In the markup the ✕ comes last, so the first focus stays the form's. Cancel stays beside a form's main button and closes it at once.

On the Desk: every dialog, the design workspace, the enlarged picture and the private ask; a dialog with a header that stays (an element's) holds its ✕ there.

The toast

What just happened, once

At the bottom, for a few seconds, after an action whose result does not show where you are. Never a question, never an error that needs a move.

Tokens

Type

Picked by what the text is. Medium weight for a control's label, semibold for names and emphasis. The scale is in The look.

--fs-title an item's or a page's name

--fs-head a section, a dialog's name

--fs-ui text to read, controls, lists

--fs-meta dates, who, counts

--fs-micro badges, tiny labels

Corners

Two corners, by what the thing is. Small for what you press or what sits inside something else; large for a surface that holds things. Beyond them only two shapes: a pill, round at its ends, and a circle.

--r-smbuttons, fields, menus and their items, tags, pictures, a control's parts
--radiuscards, panels, dialogs, what floats
999pxa pill: a chip, a state, a count
50%a dot, an avatar

Shadow

Most things sit flat on a line. A shadow only for a card that stands out, and a deeper one for what floats over the page.
